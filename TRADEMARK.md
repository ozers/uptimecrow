# Trademark Policy

The UptimeCrow **source code** is licensed under AGPL-3.0 (see [LICENSE](./LICENSE)).
The UptimeCrow **name, logo, and crow mark** are not. A software licence grants
rights to the code; it says nothing about the brand.

This page exists so nobody has to guess where the line is.

## What you can always do

- Fork the code, modify it, run it anywhere, for anything — including commercially.
- Say your project "is based on UptimeCrow", "is a fork of UptimeCrow", or "is
  compatible with UptimeCrow". Accurate, factual references are fair use and we
  encourage them.
- Use the name in blog posts, talks, comparisons, tutorials, and reviews.
- Keep the branding intact when you self-host an **unmodified** build for your
  own organisation. Running our image on your server does not require a rename.

## What needs a different name

- **Redistributing a modified build under the UptimeCrow name.** If you change
  the code and hand it to other people, give it your own name and logo. Users
  need to know whose software they are running when something breaks.
- **Offering a hosted service called "UptimeCrow"** (or a confusingly similar
  name — "UptimeCrow Cloud", "UptimeCrow Pro", "Uptime Crow"). Run the service,
  charge for it, that is what AGPL allows; just do it under your own brand.
- **Using the crow mark as your product's logo**, or any mark close enough that
  a reasonable person would think it came from us.

## Domain names and social handles

Do not register domains or accounts whose primary element is "uptimecrow" or a
close variant, in a way that suggests you are the official project.

## Attribution when you rename

If you fork and rename, a line like this in your README is welcome but not
required:

> Based on [UptimeCrow](https://github.com/ozers/uptimecrow), AGPL-3.0.

Note that AGPL-3.0 itself requires you to keep the copyright notices and offer
your users the corresponding source. This policy is on top of the licence, not
instead of it.

Anything not covered here, or a use you are unsure about: email

to a described plan than argue about it afterwards.
